PaxVR for Deventer Hospital

As part of the IMT&S semeser in the fourth year of Creative Media & Game Technologies, I have been working on a calming VR application for Deventer Hospital. I had the role of engineer in our six person team, which means I worked on creating the functionality in Unreal Engine, using blueprints.

The project

The client at Deventer Hospital asked for a VR application that can be used by patients when they are undergoing small procedures, for example blood drawing. The application should be calming and distracting, to divert the patients attention from anything that makes them nervous.

Interactions

I have been working on VR interactions that only involve the movement of the head. To keep the application as simple as possible, the client requested to not use any controllers for interactions. This limits the interaction possibilites quite a bit. I have created a 'gaze pointer', which the user can use to point at objects (similar to a crosshair), and confirm their choice after a certain duration.

A combination of blueprints, widget blueprints and smart use of line tracing and scaling resulted in a gaze pointer that is easy to focus on in terms of depth, and can be used to interact with objects.

Minigames

I have implemented the two minigames that were designed to be in our project. The first one is the 'Porch Defender' game, where the player has to defend their three bells from being stolen by ducks. The player can shoot/feed the ducks with grapes to distract them.

The second one is the Constellations game, where the player plays connnect the dots with stars, to form constellations.

Building for the Pico Headset - Android

Our client was going to use the application on the Pico G2 4K VR headset specifically. This is not a very high-end headset, which runs on android. To create a succesful product, we had to build a lightweight android application. It was a challenge to keep it optimized enough to run smoothly, but it teached me a lot about performance and profiling in Unreal Engine.
